A 10-minute video from Mobbin, which sells a library of app screenshots. It
tags 1,460 onboarding flows from 986 apps in that library and argues that the
goal is getting people to value quickly, not keeping onboarding short. It
walks through patterns using screenshots from real apps and backs five of
them with vendor-reported A/B tests: Headspace, Dollar Shave Club, Houzz,
Mural, and Grammarly. None of those reports gives a sample size or
significance, and the video misquotes two of them: Grammarly's lift and the
number of steps in Mural's checklist.

**Skipped:**

- **Counts of how common each pattern is** (25 screens on average, 23% of
  apps personalize, 22% show a paywall). These are Mobbin's own tallies and
  say what is common, not what works.
- **"The most successful apps have the longest flows."** This is a
  correlation with survivorship bias. Finance apps run long because the law
  requires identity checks.
- **Conversational tone** (Dollar Shave Club, +5.24%). The only evidence is
  one agency case study with no sample size.
- **Adding delight to long flows.** The host's taste; nothing was measured.
- **One-time offers to drive urgency.** No evidence was given, and false
  urgency is a recognized dark pattern.
- **Social proof before the paywall.** No evidence was given.
- **"Eastern markets prefer dense UIs."** An unsupported generalization.
