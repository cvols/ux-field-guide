---
title: Teach in context, not up front
summary: Put guidance where the task happens, such as a tip at the step or an example in an empty state, instead of a tutorial before people start.
category: onboarding
principle: none
confidence: established
sources: [yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU]
---

**Do:** Explain a step at the step, in a line or two that says why it
matters. Give empty states example content and a nudge toward the first
action. If you need a checklist, use one that stays available and can be
dismissed.

**Don't:** Open with a deck of tutorial cards. Don't stack pop-ups, banners,
and tours that each push a different action.

**Why:** Instructions given before people need them are forgotten, and a
tutorial can make a simple app look hard.

**Notes:** Nielsen Norman Group tested deck-of-cards tutorials with 70 people
on 4 iOS apps. People who read the tutorial were no more successful (91% vs
94%, not significant) and rated the tasks harder (4.92 vs 5.49 on a 7-point
scale, p = 0.047). It recommends contextual help instead
(https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/). yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU (6:45)
cites a Mural test in which one checklist replaced competing prompts and
1-week retention rose 10%. That number comes from a growth newsletter with
no sample size, and the video says the checklist had six steps where the
sources say five.
