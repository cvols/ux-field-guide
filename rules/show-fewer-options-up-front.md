---
title: Show fewer options up front
summary: When people must pick one of many options, lead with a short, curated set and let them expand to the rest.
category: choice
principle: Choice overload
confidence: disputed
sources: [ig-Del7XTgsuwI, yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU]
---

**Do:** Lead with a few recommended or popular options, and offer filters or
"show all" for the rest. This matters most when the options are hard to
compare, none is clearly best, or people do not know what they want.

**Don't:** Open with a large grid of similar options and no guidance.

**Why:** A long list of similar options makes the decision harder, and some
people put it off.

**Spot it:**

- In code: a decision point where one item must be picked (plans, products,
  a category to start with) that renders more than about eight options at
  once, with no recommended or popular marker, filter, or "show more".
  Lists of content to browse, such as a feed or a schedule, are not decision
  points.
- On screen: a large, uniform grid of choices at a decision point.

**Notes:** ig-Del7XTgsuwI (1:17) cites the jam study (Iyengar and Lepper,
2000): 24 jams sold to 3% of shoppers who stopped, and 6 jams to 30%. That
finding has replicated poorly. A meta-analysis by Scheibehenne, Greifeneder,
and Todd (2010) pooled 50 experiments and found a mean effect near zero, with
large variation between studies. Choice overload seems to appear only under
some conditions, such as complex choices or no dominant option. Treat this as
a trade-off to test, not a law.
