---
title: Only ask questions that change the experience
summary: Keep onboarding questions few, let people pick every goal that applies, and show right away what their answers changed.
category: onboarding
principle: none
confidence: single-source
sources: [yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU]
---

**Do:** Ask only questions whose answers change what people get. Let them
pick more than one goal. Follow the questions with something built from the
answers, such as a plan or a home screen already filled with relevant
content.

**Don't:** Ask questions whose answers change nothing. Don't force a single
choice when people have several needs. Don't turn the answers into precise
promises you cannot back, such as an exact date to reach a weight goal.

**Why:** Questions feel worth answering when people can see the payoff.

**Spot it:**

- In code: onboarding answers that are stored but never read anywhere else
  (search for each saved field); single-select controls for goals that can
  overlap.
- On screen: nothing visibly changes after the questions are answered.

**Notes:** yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU (2:32) cites Headspace's change to multi-select
goals as a "nearly 10%" lift in free-trial conversion. The source is a
Headspace product director quoted in a newsletter, with no sample size and
no statement of whether the lift is relative or absolute
(https://www.insidergrowthhq.com/p/3-case-studies-from-headspace-on).
