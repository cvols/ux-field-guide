---
title: State the real cost of declining, without shaming
summary: When ignoring a prompt has a real consequence, name it plainly, and never invent a loss or guilt-trip the decline button.
category: content
principle: Loss aversion
confidence: established
sources: [yt-2TlIg3VokY8]
---

**Do:** Say what actually happens if people decline: which files, when, and
how to avoid it, including free options. Label the decline button neutrally,
such as "Not now".

**Don't:** Make up deadlines or losses to sell an upgrade. Don't label the
decline with guilt, such as "I'll risk it" or "No, I don't care about my
files". That pattern is called confirmshaming.

**Why:** Concrete consequences help people decide. Threats and shaming wear
down trust and win little in return.

**Notes:** deceptive.design catalogues confirmshaming as a deceptive pattern
that uses guilt or shame to push people into something they would not
otherwise do (https://www.deceptive.design/types/confirmshaming).
Meta-analyses of message framing by O'Keefe and Jensen (2007, 2009) found
little or no advantage for loss-framed messages over gain-framed ones.
yt-2TlIg3VokY8 (8:20) says "threat wins every time" and shows "I'll Risk It"
as the better decline label. This rule rejects both.
