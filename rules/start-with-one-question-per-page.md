---
title: Start with one question per page
summary: In long forms and sign-up flows, start with one question or decision per screen, and merge screens only when research shows people can handle more.
category: forms
principle: none
confidence: established
sources: [yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU]
---

**Do:** Split a long form so each screen asks one thing: one question, one
decision, or one closely related set of fields such as a name. Merge screens
when testing shows people move through them easily.

**Don't:** Assume fewer screens always means less friction.

**Why:** One thing per screen helps people understand what you are asking,
focus on the answer, recover from errors, and use the form on a phone.

**Spot it:**

- In code: a sign-up, checkout, or setup form on mobile with more than about
  six inputs on one screen.
- On screen: a mobile form needs scrolling to reach its submit button.

**Notes:** GOV.UK's service manual recommends starting this way and merging
pages when user research supports it
(https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure).
yt-Qsq-Sj_rojU (8:24) cites Houzz splitting contact details across screens
for a 15% lift in conversions. That number comes from a newsletter with no
sample size, and the fields also moved later in the flow, which may account
for the change (https://abtest.design/tests/separating-contact-information-into-multiple-screens).
